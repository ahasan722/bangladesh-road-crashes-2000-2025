Every day, about twenty people in Bangladesh leave home and die on the road.

Twenty-five years of crash records from the police, two media-monitoring groups and the WHO disagree with each other by a factor of six. This explorer puts all of them on the same axis, maps where the deaths cluster, walks the deadliest highways kilometre by kilometre, and asks who dies, when, and on which roads.

Chapter 1

Nobody agrees how many die

Police count first-information reports. Jatri Kalyan Samity and the Road Safety Foundation count what newspapers print. The WHO models the total from health data. Toggle the sources and watch the same country produce four different death tolls.

Chapter 7

Method, sources, limits

Everything on this page comes from documents anyone can download. Nothing is modelled or interpolated by me. Where sources disagree, both are shown. Where data does not exist, the chart says so instead of pretending.

Read this before you quote a number

  • This is an independent learning project by one person. It is not a product of, endorsed by, or affiliated with the Bangladesh Police, BRTA, the Roads and Highways Department, LGED, ARI-BUET, Bangladesh Jatri Kalyan Samity, the Road Safety Foundation, the WHO, or my employer.
  • All figures are secondary data copied from published reports and press coverage of them. Transcription errors are possible; every number links to its source so you can check.
  • Police figures count registered cases. Jatri Kalyan Samity and Road Safety Foundation figures count crashes reported in the media. WHO figures are statistical estimates. They measure different things and should never be added together or compared without that caveat.
  • The highway corridor view counts hazardous locations listed by RHD, the Highway Police, ARI-BUET researchers and the Road Safety Foundation, located to the nearest named place or, where nothing finer was published, the upazila. It shows where lists cluster. It does not show crashes per vehicle-kilometre, and absence of a bar does not mean a stretch is safe.
  • Highway alignments come from OpenStreetMap (trunk and primary roads). Kilometre positions are measured along that line and will differ from RHD chainage by a few kilometres.
  • No individual crash, victim or vehicle is identified anywhere on this page.
